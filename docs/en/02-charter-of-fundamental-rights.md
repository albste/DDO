# DDO Charter of Fundamental Rights

### The non-votable core of the Direct Decentralized Organization

by **albste** · Oct 17, 2026 · Version 0.1 · License: CC BY-SA 4.0

Companion to the DDO Whitepaper.

## Preamble

In DDO, citizens decide directly. Direct decision-making is only legitimate if it cannot be used to strip people of the conditions that make free decision possible. This Charter lists the few rights that no ordinary vote, weighted or not, can remove. It is deliberately short: every article added to it narrows what citizens can decide, so each one must justify that cost.

The Charter is drafted to be compatible with established international human rights instruments, and each pilot community is expected to check it against the law that applies to it.

## Part I. Rights of the person

**Article 1. Equal dignity and non-discrimination.** Every person has equal dignity. No law, vote or procedure may treat a person worse because of origin, sex, gender, language, belief, disability, age, social condition or similar characteristics. Differences in voting weight under the DDO scoring model are allowed only where they follow from verified competence or involvement in the domain of the law, never from these characteristics.

**Article 2. Freedom of conscience, belief and expression.** Every person may hold and express opinions and beliefs, and may criticize any proposal, role holder or rule of the system, including this Charter.

**Article 3. Privacy and protection from surveillance.** Every person has a right to private life. Personal data may be collected only when strictly necessary, kept only as long as needed, and disclosed only in the minimum form required by a recorded legal basis. Every access to a person's data by an authority is visible to that person and in the public audit log. Mass or indiscriminate surveillance is not permitted.

**Article 4. Due process.** Everyone is presumed innocent until proven otherwise, has the right to know the reasons for any measure against them, to be heard, and to appeal to an independent reviewer. Sanctions in the system, including removal from a role or suspension from the platform, follow this article.

**Article 5. Integrity of the person.** No vote may authorize torture, cruel or degrading treatment, forced labour or slavery.

## Part II. Rights of participation

**Article 6. Equal baseline voice.** Every citizen in good standing has the same baseline weight in every vote. Extra weight, where the system allows it, is capped, domain-specific, non-transferable and never purchasable. No citizen in good standing may be deprived of the baseline vote except through the due process of Article 4.

**Article 7. Right to propose.** Any citizen may propose an idea in any field of social life, without discrimination. Reaching the support threshold, not the identity or status of the proposer, decides whether a proposal goes to a vote.

**Article 8. Freedom of association and assembly.** Citizens may gather, organize and campaign for or against any proposal, peacefully and without prior approval.

**Article 9. Access and inclusion.** Nobody may be excluded from participating because of lack of devices, connection, digital skills, disability or remote residence. The system must provide public kiosks, assisted voting and offline enrolment.

**Article 10. Integrity of the voting system.** Votes are secret and cannot be linked to the voter. Every voter can verify that their vote was counted as cast, and anyone can verify that the result matches the recorded votes. Each eligible person has one counted vote per question. No one may be pressured, paid or deceived into voting a certain way.

## Part III. Limits on power

**Article 11. No standing rulers.** Mediators and operators have no decision power of their own. No office may hold authority beyond its term, and no one may extend a term without a citizen vote.

**Article 12. Transparency and open verification.** Rules, code, scoring methods and public records are open to inspection. Any citizen may challenge a decision, score or record in a public review procedure.

**Article 13. Protection of minorities in decisions.** No law may remove the eligibility of a group to vote or to propose, nor target a group with a penalty or burden on the basis of the characteristics listed in Article 1. Laws affecting a minority directly must be open to proposals from members of that minority.

## Part IV. Enforcement and amendment

**Article 14. Supremacy.** Any ordinary law that conflicts with this Charter is void.

**Article 15. Challenge and review.** Any citizen may challenge a law as contrary to the Charter. Challenges are examined by a review panel selected by lot from eligible citizens for a short fixed term, which publishes reasoned opinions. Its opinions can be appealed to the full body of citizens under the amendment procedure below. Whether this panel is the best mechanism is listed as an open question.

**Article 16. Amendment.** This Charter can be amended, because everything in DDO is revisable, but only through a stricter procedure:

- every eligible citizen has an equal vote, with no domain scores;
- the proposal needs a supermajority and a minimum turnout, both fixed in the founding vote;
- two votes separated by a long, fixed delay are required;
- the amendment procedure itself can only be changed by following that same procedure;
- Articles 1, 5, 6 and 10 are the minimum core; any change touching them requires the highest supermajority set in the founding vote.

## Open questions

- Is a lot-selected review panel enough to resolve disputes about the Charter, or does it become a hidden power centre?
- Which rights are missing, and which listed here are too broad to justify limiting citizens' decisions?
- How should the Charter relate to a host country's own constitution during pilots?
- Where should the line sit between protecting a minority and blocking a legitimate majority decision?
